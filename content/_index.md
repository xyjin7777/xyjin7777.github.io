## Research

My research focuses on cosmic reionization, the last major phase transition of the diffuse gas between galaxies, the intergalactic medium (IGM). During reionization, the neutral hydrogen in the IGM was ionized by UV photons from the first luminous sources in the universe.

To study reionization, I examine the connections between galaxies, AGN, and the IGM, using the quasar Lyα forest as a probe. The Lyα forest is a series of absorption features blueswards of the quasar Lyα emission line, and it traces IGM transmission along hundreds of comoving Mpc in the line of sight direction. Only highly ionized regions in the IGM would allow transmission, showing up as transmission spikes, whereas regions with a neutral fraction above 0.1% appear as dark absorption troughs in the quasar Ly-alpha forest.

**IGM transmission near galaxies**

<span class="card-meta">JWST ASPIRE · JWST GO 3325</span>

We measure the IGM transmission as a function of distance to [OIII]-emitting galaxies in 5 ASPIRE quasar fields, each observed with a single NIRCam pointing, and in one quasar field with multiple NIRCam observations. In both stuides, we find a Lyα absorption within 5-10 cMpc of the galaxies, and a 2-sigma excess IGM transmission at 10-40 cMpc, compared with the cosmic average. This suggests that the regions traced by these galaxies contribute to the local ionizing background, resulting in an excess IGM transmission. We model the ionizing photon production of the observed galaxies and find they cannot produce strong excess transmission even with an 100% ionizing photon escape fraction. Faint, undetected galaxies must also contribute to the local ionizing background. 

For more details, see [Jin et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14959) and [Kakiichi, Jin et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07074)

**IGM transmission near and far from galaxies**

<span class="card-meta">JWST ASPIRE</span>

We measure the IGM effective optical depth (the optical depth of large-scale IGM transmission, measured in 50 cMpc/h bins) of IGM patches near [OIII]-emitting galaxies and farther from [OIII]-emitting galaxies at z<6. We find that regions near the [OIII]-emitting galaxies reach a given effective optical depth at least 0.1 in redshift (>=20 Myr in cosmic time) earlier than regions farther from [OIII]-emitting galaxies. This suggests that reionization happended first near the [OIII]-emitting galaxies, and later proceeded to the underdense regions without [OIII]-emitting galaxies, consistent with an "inside-out" topology of reionization.

For more details, please check [Jin et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/ad82de)

**IGM transmission near AGN**

<span class="card-meta">JWST ASPIRE · JWST GO 3325</span>

JWST has discovered abundant broad-line AGN among star-forming galaxies, however, their contribution to reionization remains unknown. With NIRSpec, we identify four broad-line AGN in the foreground of the ASPIRE quasar J0226+0302. We measure the IGM transmission as a function of the distance from the AGN. We find tentative evidence of excess IGM transmission within 5 cMpc of the AGN, relative to the transmission around galaxies. We develop an illustrative model of the AGN photoionization rate, and we find that an AGN ionizing photon escape fraction of 50%-100% is needed to explain such an excess transmission. If confirmed with more observations, this excess would indicate a major contribution of broad-line AGN to reionization. 


For more details, please check [Jin et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14959)

**Finding dark regions in the Ly-alpha/beta forest to constrain the timing of reionization**

<span class="card-meta">Ground-based Optical Spectroscopy · z ~ 5-7 </span>

Dark regions in the Lyα forest trace neutral gas, but their exact neutral fraction is unclear. By assuming that those dark regions fully neutral (100% neutral fraction), we obtain upper limits on the IGM neutral fraction. We measure the dark fractions of the Lyα and Lyβ forest of 53 quasars and derive upper limits on the IGM neutral fraction from z=5.1-6.8, filling a gap in previous IGM neutral fraction measurements from quasars. Our results show that reionization was still ongoing at z<6, and the IGM was not completely neutral at z=6.7, consistent with Planck 2018 results.

For more details, please check [Jin et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/aca678)
